CORPORATE OVERVIEW & STRATEGIC HIGHLIGHTS
Solaris Power Tech Limited is one of India's leading renewable energy engineering and utility-scale solar infrastructure providers. 
During FY 2024-25, the Company achieved significant operational scale across utility solar parks, rooftop commercial installations, and grid battery storage solutions.
For the financial year ended March 31, 2025, consolidated revenue from operations reached ₹4,850 crore, marking an 18.5% year-on-year growth compared to ₹4,092 crore in FY 2023-24.
Consolidated EBITDA stood at ₹1,120 crore, representing an EBITDA margin of 23.1%, expanding by 140 basis points over the previous fiscal period.
The Company's unexecuted order book as of March 31, 2025 stood at a record ₹11,400 crore, providing strong revenue visibility for the next 24 to 30 months.

--- PAGE 2 ---
FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE & SEGMENTAL HIGHLIGHTS
Utility Scale EPC Division contributed ₹3,200 crore to operating revenues with segment EBIT margin of 19.5%.
Commercial & Industrial (C&I) Rooftop segment delivered ₹1,150 crore with segment EBIT margin of 26.2%.
Consolidated Profit After Tax (PAT) expanded by 22.4% year-on-year to ₹625 crore.
The Board of Directors recommended a final dividend of ₹3.50 per equity share of face value ₹2 each.
Net cash flow from operations for the fiscal year remained resilient at ₹810 crore, supporting ongoing internal capital expenditure.
Management guidance for FY 2025-26 projects consolidated revenue growth between 16% and 20%, underpinned by central grid tenders and state utility bidding pipelines.

RISK FACTORS & OPERATIONAL CHALLENGES
1. Raw Material Volatility: Solar module cells and high-grade polysilicon prices remain subject to international tariff fluctuations and supply-chain shipping freight spikes.
2. Working Capital Cycle: The average debtor collection days increased from 72 days in FY 2023-24 to 88 days in FY 2024-25 due to delayed state discom milestone disbursements.
3. Debt and Borrowing Maturity: Total consolidated outstanding debt as of March 31, 2025 was ₹2,150 crore, of which ₹480 crore matures for refinancing in FY 2026-27.
4. Competitive Intensity: Tariff bidding in central tenders has witnessed aggressive participation from domestic conglomerates, potentially putting long-term tariff yields under pressure.
5. Foreign Exchange Exposure: Approximately 35% of total capital component procurement is denominated in USD, creating currency fluctuation exposure partially mitigated by derivative hedges.
