FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE OVERVIEW — Q2 FY 2024-25
Tata Motors Limited reported consolidated revenue from operations of ₹101,450 crore for Q2 FY25, representing a 1.8% year-on-year increase compared to ₹99,650 crore in Q2 FY24.
Consolidated EBITDA for the quarter stood at ₹15,700 crore, delivering an operating EBITDA margin of 15.5%.
Profit Before Tax (PBT) before exceptional items reached ₹7,600 crore, reflecting continued execution discipline and cost management across automotive operations.
Consolidated Profit After Tax (PAT) for the quarter was reported at ₹3,450 crore.
Free cash flow for the automotive business in H1 FY25 remained positive at ₹4,100 crore, enabling ongoing balance-sheet deleveraging.

--- PAGE 2 ---
SEGMENTAL EXECUTION & COMMERCIAL METRICS
1. Jaguar Land Rover (JLR): JLR delivered revenue of £6.5 billion in Q2 FY25, with an EBIT margin of 5.6%. Demand for modern luxury vehicles remained strong, led by Range Rover, Range Rover Sport, and Defender, which accounted for 67% of the order backlog.
2. Commercial Vehicles (Tata CV): Tata Motors Commercial Vehicles business reported Q2 FY25 revenue of ₹17,300 crore. Operating EBITDA margin for commercial vehicles expanded to 10.8%, supported by higher fleet utilization, realization improvement, and infrastructure demand.
3. Passenger Vehicles (Tata PV): Revenue stood at ₹11,700 crore. Electric Vehicle (EV) penetration reached 12% of total domestic passenger car sales, solidifying market leadership in domestic clean mobility.

--- PAGE 3 ---
BALANCE SHEET DELEVERAGING & DEBT PROFILE
Consolidated net automotive debt was successfully reduced to ₹22,000 crore as of September 30, 2024, down significantly from ₹29,200 crore reported at the end of FY 2023-24.
Finance costs decreased by 14% year-on-year to ₹2,100 crore, directly benefiting from ongoing debt repayments and favorable loan refinancing terms.
The Company remains firmly committed to achieving near net zero automotive debt by the end of FY 2024-25.
Capital expenditures for FY25 are budgeted at ₹35,000 crore, primarily directed towards JLR electrification architecture (EMA platform) and domestic EV powertrain localization.

--- PAGE 4 ---
RISK FACTORS & OPERATIONAL CHALLENGES
1. Geopolitical Shipping Vulnerability: Disruption in Red Sea logistics routes has lengthened supplier transit lead times by 10 to 14 days and raised maritime container freight premiums.
2. Commodity Price Volatility: Aluminum, high-grade specialty steel, and rare-earth magnet costs remain subject to trade tariff changes and global spot market volatility.
3. European Regulatory Emissions: Stricter CAFE targets in the UK and European Union necessitate accelerated battery electric vehicle (BEV) rollout to avoid regulatory compliance penalties.
